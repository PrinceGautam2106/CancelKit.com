Few emails spike blood pressure like a receipt for a **free trial you forgot**. The charge already posted. Now what?

This is the calm, same-day playbook.

## 1. Confirm it is really a trial conversion

Check:

- Merchant name / descriptor  
- Exact amount  
- Whether it says “renewal,” “subscription,” or “membership”  

Sometimes the charge is sales tax on a separate purchase, a family-plan seat, or a different product from the same company.

## 2. Cancel immediately (even if you want a refund)

Canceling stops **next** month. Refunds are a separate conversation.

Cancel in the same place you subscribed:

- **iOS trial:** Settings → Subscriptions  
- **Android trial:** Google Play subscriptions  
- **Website trial:** account billing page  

If you only delete the app, you may still be billed.

## 3. Gather proof before you chat support

Have ready:

- Screenshot of trial signup terms (if you still have the email)  
- Cancellation confirmation  
- Bank line item  

Polite + specific wins more refunds than rage.

## 4. Request a refund the smart way

Message templates that work:

> “I was on a free trial that converted on [date]. I cancelled on [date] and have not used the paid product. Please refund the charge of [$x] and confirm cancellation.”

Try merchant support first. App Store / Google Play also have purchase report flows for many digital goods.

## 5. If the merchant refuses

Options:

- Escalate once with clearer proof  
- Dispute with your bank as a last resort (know this can affect account access)  
- At minimum, ensure cancellation is confirmed so the damage is one month, not twelve  

## 6. Prevent the next trial trap

Rules of thumb:

- Put a calendar alert for **day 5 of a 7-day trial**  
- Use virtual cards with spend limits when available  
- Prefer trials that do not require a card (rare, but best)  
- Track trials in one list the day you start them
