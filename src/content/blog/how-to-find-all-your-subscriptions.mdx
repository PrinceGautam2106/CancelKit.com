If you have ever opened a bank app and thought **“what is this charge?”**, you are not alone. The average household juggles streaming, software, fitness, news, cloud storage, and free trials that quietly turned into paid plans.

Here is a clear way to **find all your subscriptions** in one afternoon — then decide what stays and what dies.

## 1. Start with your bank and cards (last 90 days)

Pull statements for every debit card, credit card, and digital wallet you use.

Search for words like:

- `subscription`
- `membership`
- `premium`
- `plus`
- `recurring`

Also scan for familiar brands: Netflix, Spotify, Adobe, Apple, Google, Amazon, Microsoft, gym chains, meal kits, VPNs.

**Tip:** Export CSV from your bank if you can. Sorting by merchant name exposes duplicates (same service billed under slightly different descriptors).

## 2. Hunt email receipts

Open Gmail / Outlook and search:

```
subject:(receipt OR invoice OR "your membership" OR "renewal" OR "subscription")
```

Filter to the last 12 months. Open anything you do not recognize and note:

1. Service name  
2. Monthly / annual price  
3. Renewal date  
4. Cancel link (if present)

Many “mystery charges” are annual renewals you approved once and forgot.

## 3. Check App Store and Google Play

Phone billings often bypass your memory because they look like Apple or Google charges.

- **iPhone:** Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions  
- **Android:** Play Store → profile → Payments & subscriptions → Subscriptions  

Cancel anything you do not use weekly. Trials auto-renewing here are a top panic source.

## 4. Review Apple One, Amazon, Microsoft, and Google account hubs

These hubs hide multiple products under one login:

- Apple Subscriptions (Music, TV+, iCloud+, Arcade, News+)
- Amazon Memberships & Subscriptions (Prime, Audible, Kindle Unlimited, channels)
- Microsoft account services (365, Game Pass, LinkedIn if bundled)
- Google One / YouTube / Play Pass

If you only cancel one app, a sibling product may keep billing.

## 5. Look at payroll, benefits, and “perks”

Employer tools sometimes deduct:

- wellness apps  
- commuting credits that became paid  
- learning platforms  

Check HR portals and benefit dashboards once a year.

## 6. Build a simple inventory

Make a list with five columns:

| Service | $/mo | Next bill | Used weekly? | Keep / Cancel |
|---------|------|-----------|--------------|---------------|

Be honest on “used weekly.” If the answer is no, it is a cancel candidate.

## What to do after you find them

1. Cancel the easy wins first (unused streaming / trials).  
2. Pause annual plans before renewal week.  
3. Keep one “entertainment” and one “productivity” stack if you need constraints.  
4. Set calendar alerts 7 days before renewals you keep.
