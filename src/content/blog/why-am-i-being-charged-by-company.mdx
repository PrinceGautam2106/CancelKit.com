A strange line on your statement triggers a very specific panic: **“Why am I being charged by this company?”**

Slow down. Most mystery charges are forgotten subscriptions, free trials that converted, or billing descriptors that do not match the brand name you remember. Use this checklist before you dispute everything in sight.

## Step 1: Read the descriptor carefully

Banks shorten names. Examples:

- `GOOGLE *Some App` → Google Play subscription  
- `APL*APPLE.COM/BILL` → Apple subscription  
- `AMZNDigital` / `AUDIBLE` → Amazon digital services  
- `FACEBK` → often ads, sometimes Meta verified / boosts  

Search the exact descriptor string in Google plus the word `subscription` or `charge`.

## Step 2: Match the amount and date

Ask:

- Is it the same amount every month / year?  
- Did a free trial end around this date?  
- Did you buy something once that became a membership?

Recurring amounts are almost always subscriptions. One-offs may be late shipping, tips, or usage overages.

## Step 3: Check the big billing hubs first

Before calling the bank:

1. Apple Subscriptions  
2. Google Play subscriptions  
3. Amazon Memberships & Subscriptions  
4. PayPal / Venmo automatic payments  
5. Microsoft account services  

A huge share of “unknown companies” are actually apps billed through these platforms.

## Step 4: Search your email for the merchant

Try:

```
"thank you for subscribing" OR "your trial ends" OR "payment successful" OR "invoice"
```

Add part of the descriptor. Old welcome emails often include the cancel URL you need.

## Step 5: Identify → cancel → confirm

Once you know the product:

1. Cancel in the correct portal (app store vs website vs phone).  
2. Save confirmation emails / screenshots.  
3. Watch the next statement cycle.

Use our [cancel guides](/cancel) for step-by-step help on major services.

## Step 6: Ask for a refund (when it makes sense)

Good candidates for a refund request:

- Charged after you cancelled with proof  
- Trial converted without a clear reminder and you act quickly  
- Duplicate charges  

Call or chat with the merchant first. Banks can dispute, but merchants resolve many cases faster if you still have account access.

## Step 7: Harden your cards

After cleanup:

- Remove unused cards from streaming apps  
- Turn on purchase notifications  
- Use virtual card numbers for trials when your bank supports them  
- Track subscriptions so the next mystery charge never happens
